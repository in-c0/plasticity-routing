\title{Learning Where to Write:\\
A Controlled Proof-of-Principle for Resource-Aware\\
Write-Depth Routing in Continual Agents}
\author{Ava Kim}
\date{2026-09-04}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Continual agents are usually given a fixed pathway along which experience is
committed to memory. We ask whether an agent can instead \emph{learn} which
substrate a new experience should change, from delayed future task utility,
under interference and storage/compute constraints. We formalise this as
resource-aware write-depth allocation over four actions --- \textsc{ignore},
\textsc{episodic}, \textsc{fast} and \textsc{slow} --- and introduce SDW-1, a
synthetic stream in which four hidden classes genuinely deserve different
storage depths and are observationally identical on first encounter. On five
held-out seeds, a policy reading only decision-time-legal features beats the
best fixed rule found under an \emph{equal search budget} by
$+0.0907$ $[+0.0798, +0.1016]$, positive on 5/5 seeds, landing $0.042$ below a
class-conditional oracle ceiling. A cross-world negative control shows the
advantage depends on training having seen a genuine prefix-to-future-utility
relationship: an identically specified policy trained where that relationship
was destroyed scores $+0.0201$ against $+0.5130$ on the same worlds, and the
crossover interaction is $+0.6011$ $[+0.5902, +0.6122]$. We report this as a
\emph{controlled proof-of-principle on one synthetic benchmark with five seeds},
not as a broad empirical demonstration, and we make no language-model claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A continual learner must decide not only \emph{what} to learn but \emph{where}
the change should land. Most systems answer that question by construction: a
designer fixes the pathway --- write to an external store, or fine-tune, or
consolidate on a schedule --- and the agent's only freedom is whether and how
much. This paper asks whether the destination itself can be learned.

The contribution is deliberately narrow. We do \emph{not} claim that routing to
experts is new; sparsely-gated mixture-of-experts routing is a decade-old line
\cite{shazeer2017moe,fedus2021switch,zhou2025twolevel,guo2026stablerouting}. We
do not claim that using multiple memory systems is new; complementary learning
systems and multi-timescale memory are long established, with recent instances
in \cite{behrouz2024titans,pattichis2026multitimescale,tiwari2026fastslow} and
the clearest statement of \emph{why} the systems are complementary in
\cite{lampinen2025latent}. We do not claim that deciding whether to write to
memory is new \cite{yan2025memoryr1,simsek2026forget,hu2026memrouter,jaber2026memoir}.

The question we isolate is whether a policy can learn a \emph{resource-aware
write-depth} policy from delayed future utility, and --- critically --- whether
any resulting gain is attributable to routing rather than to added capacity,
added compute, or access to privileged task identity.

\paragraph{Prior.} Our prior on a positive result was low. Yoon
\cite{yoon2026subspace} reports that a simple similarity rule matches or beats a
learned reinforcement-learning allocation controller under fixed capacity,
concluding that the memory mechanism rather than a learned allocation policy
drives continual-learning performance. We therefore preregistered a fixed
heuristic as the \emph{primary} comparator and a negative replication as a
publishable outcome.

\section{Related work and the surviving claim}

Four clusters bound the contribution.

\textbf{Expert and module routing.} Progressive networks \cite{rusu2016progressive},
PathNet \cite{fernando2017pathnet}, dynamically expandable networks
\cite{yoon2017den} and CN-DPM \cite{lee2020cndpm} route \emph{which module
computes} or \emph{when to add capacity}. Their action space is module identity;
ours is persistence depth.

\textbf{Learned plasticity.} Differentiable plasticity \cite{miconi2018diffplasticity},
Backpropamine \cite{miconi2020backpropamine}, NeuroPlastic
\cite{jiang2026neuroplastic} and GateRA \cite{ou2025gatera} learn a continuous,
differentiable \emph{magnitude} of change within one parametric substrate,
driven by immediate loss. They do not make a discrete allocation across
substrates with different persistence and different resource prices.

\textbf{Agent memory write policies.} Memory-R1 \cite{yan2025memoryr1} trains
ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP over external memory; MemRL \cite{zhang2026memrl} and
RoMeRL \cite{yang2026romerl} learn episodic-memory utilities. All fix the
substrate and vary the operation. BudgetMem \cite{zhang2026budgetmem} performs
RL budget-tier routing with explicit performance-cost control, but its tiers are
levels of memory-\emph{construction compute}; every write lands in the same
external store and no parameters are updated.

\textbf{The closest work.} Dual-Layer Agentic Memory \cite{li2026duallayer}
performs cost-aware epistemic routing at the write phase (non-write /
write-new / write-update) and separately consolidates external memory into
parameters by periodic supervised fine-tuning. Three gaps remain. (i) The routed
decision is whether to externalise, taken \emph{within} the external substrate;
the external-to-parametric depth transition is a \emph{schedule}, not a per-item
routed decision. (ii) The router is a prompted model cascade judging epistemic
redundancy, not a policy trained from delayed future task utility. (iii) There
is no single objective in which write cost, storage occupancy, compute and
interference-driven forgetting are commensurable and jointly optimised.

\paragraph{Surviving claim.} Can a policy learn, from delayed future task
utility under a unified resource budget, to allocate each experience to a
substrate of differing persistence and interference character --- and is any
gain attributable to routing rather than to capacity, compute, or privileged
task identity? No single property below is novel alone; the claim is the
conjunction plus the controls that make it measurable.

\section{Problem formulation}

At each write event $t$ the agent observes $(k_t, v_t)$ and selects
$a_t \in \{\textsc{ignore}, \textsc{episodic}, \textsc{fast}, \textsc{slow}\}$
from $\pi_\theta(a \mid \phi_t)$, where $\phi_t$ is a function of the stream
prefix only. The objective is
\begin{equation}
J \;=\; U \;-\; \lambda_f F \;-\; \lambda_s S \;-\; \lambda_w W \;-\; \lambda_c C,
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with $U$ the mean per-query recall utility in $[0,1]$, $F$ retention regression
measured by evaluator-only probes, and $S, W, C$ normalised storage, write and
compute consumption. Frozen weights: $\lambda_f = 0.50$, $\lambda_s = 0.10$,
$\lambda_w = 0.10$, $\lambda_c = 0.05$.

\paragraph{Action space.} Four actions are the smallest set that keeps
\emph{depth} an axis. \textsc{ignore} is required, or storage cost is trivially
minimised by never routing anywhere and the noise class has no correct answer.
Two substrates would give a binary write/no-write decision, which is the setting
of \cite{yan2025memoryr1,li2026duallayer}; three ordered persistence levels are
the minimum in which ``deeper'' is meaningful and mis-routing has direction.
Latent-state and module-spawning actions are excluded: the former is a sibling
track's variable, the latter adds capacity, which is the primary confound.

Whether four actions are \emph{justified} is not assumed but tested: the
benchmark is admissible only if the empirically optimal class-to-action mapping
is a bijection (\S\ref{sec:bench}).

\paragraph{Resource accounting.} One cost table and one hard write ceiling are
shared by every arm. Writes are counted in parameter/value elements exposed to a
write; an episodic entry costs $\text{key\_dim} + \text{value\_dim}$ because it
must store its key to be retrievable. Storage is an occupancy integral. Reading
the episodic store is a similarity search costing $|E| \times \text{key\_dim}$.
Router decision compute is charged on every decision and reported separately.

\section{SDW-1}
\label{sec:bench}

Four hidden classes with different ideal depths: \textsc{noise} (never queried),
\textsc{one\_off} (queried once, soon), \textsc{local} (queried repeatedly
within a regime, value \emph{redefined} each regime), \textsc{stable} (queried
up to 2200 steps later, value fixed for the lifetime).

Three design contracts, all machine-checked. First, key and value vectors are
drawn i.i.d.\ from the same distribution for every class, so a \emph{first}
encounter carries no class information (verified by a permutation test on
per-class mean vectors). Second, recurrent keys are introduced at times drawn
uniformly over the whole lifetime and force-presented at introduction, and each
class is emitted by an independent per-step Bernoulli draw, so arrival time
carries no class information either. Third, only recurrence, value revision and
past query traffic separate the classes --- all prefix functions.

\paragraph{The ground truth was found, not asserted.} The optimal
class-to-action mapping is obtained by exhaustive search over all $4^4 = 256$
class-conditional mappings on development seeds. It is
$\textsc{noise} \to \textsc{ignore}$, $\textsc{one\_off} \to \textsc{fast}$,
$\textsc{local} \to \textsc{episodic}$, $\textsc{stable} \to \textsc{slow}$ ---
which \emph{contradicts} the mapping the benchmark was designed around
($\textsc{one\_off} \to \textsc{episodic}$, $\textsc{local} \to \textsc{fast}$),
ranked 9th of 256. We retain and report the divergence: it is evidence that the
ground truth was discovered by search rather than planted by the designer.

Admissibility requires the oracle to clear a floor, no arm to exceed a ceiling,
the oracle to beat both the best single-depth control and the heuristic by a
margin, the optimal mapping to be a bijection, and that mapping to be stable in
a local neighbourhood (15/16 points; the single boundary is
$\gamma_{\text{fast}} = 0.999$, where the fast substrate stops being a distinct
depth).

\section{Method}

The policy is a small tanh MLP over 15 whitelisted prefix features, trained by
evolution strategies on Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} directly.

\paragraph{Why ES and not policy gradients.} We first implemented REINFORCE with
per-decision credit assignment. It did not train: the development objective was
flat across 60 epochs and the policy collapsed to writing durably until the
budget was exhausted. The cause is measurable and structural --- three of the
five terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} are non-local. A write's resource cost is
$\sim$4 orders of magnitude below its attributed utility ($\sigma$ ratio
$2.6\times10^{-4}$) and vanishes under advantage standardisation; interference
caused in \emph{other} keys is not attributable to the causing write; and budget
exhaustion is a whole-trajectory property. ES optimises the objective as a black
box, requiring no attribution assumption and no reward shaping.

\paragraph{Fairness of search budget.} The learned router consumes
$3 \times 100 \times 51 = 15{,}300$ development rollouts. The three-parameter
heuristic grid consumed 81 --- a $\sim$190$\times$ asymmetry. We therefore
introduced an eight-knob fixed-rule family (adding an explicit durable-write
rule with recurrence and query-evidence conditions, and a budget guard; all
inputs still decision-time legal) and searched it with the \emph{same} 15,300
rollouts. This raised the comparator from $0.4046$ to $0.4390$ on development
seeds and is the comparator used for H1.

\paragraph{Policy selection.} Policy-seed variance dominated the ES budget: at
100 generations three initialisations spanned $0.3883 / 0.4702 / 0.5209$ on
development seeds, a spread of $0.133$, against a total budget effect of
$+0.015$ from 60 to 200 generations. We therefore train three policy seeds and
carry forward the one with the best \emph{development} objective. Selection by
confirmatory performance is prohibited.

\section{Attribution controls}

\paragraph{A control that failed.} Our first attribution control,
\textsc{random\_matched}, randomises routing while matching the learned router's
realised action histogram. It is inadequate, and we report this rather than
quietly replacing it. In a world where the future-utility schedule is randomised,
the learned policy still beat its own matched control by $+0.1357$ --- slightly
\emph{more} than the $+0.1108$ it achieved in the real world. \textsc{random\_matched}
preserves only the marginal $P(A)$; the learned router also has $P(A \mid X)$,
and generic conditional resource sense clears that bar with no utility knowledge
at all. The test is retained as permanently failed; its threshold was never
relaxed.

\paragraph{The control that replaced it.} Let $R$ be the policy trained on real
worlds and $S$ an \emph{identically specified} policy --- same network, same
feature whitelist, same ES budget, same policy seeds, same selection rule ---
trained on time-shuffled worlds in which each query's target key is redrawn
uniformly over keys already written at that moment. The write stream, query
count and query timing are byte-identical, so all budgets are unchanged; only
the prefix-to-future-utility relationship is destroyed. This is the standard
negative-control shape: the null retains every nuisance mechanism and removes
only the hypothesised informative relationship.

Evaluated once on 32 fresh audit seeds, neither training nor confirmatory
(Table~\ref{tab:l5b}). $\Delta_{\text{real}} = J_{RR} - J_{SR}$ and the crossover
interaction $I = (J_{RR}-J_{SR}) - (J_{RS}-J_{SS})$ were both required to exclude
zero on the positive side.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Five seeds.} This is the most important caveat. The preregistered
bootstrap criterion passed, so H1 is supported \emph{under the protocol}, but
20{,}000 bootstrap resamples do not turn five independent seeds into 20{,}000
observations. The bootstrap resamples the same five lifetimes; its interval
describes uncertainty about the mean of that small sample, not the breadth of
the effect. We therefore frame this as a \emph{controlled proof-of-principle},
not a broad empirical demonstration. A separately preregistered replication on
32 untouched seeds, with the frozen policy, comparator and configuration
unchanged, is the appropriate strengthening step and is specified in
\texttt{experiments/EXP-001R-PREREG.md}.

\paragraph{One synthetic benchmark.} SDW-1 is a toy with linear associative
substrates; ``interference'' here is subspace overlap, not anything a
transformer does. No language-model claim is made.

\paragraph{K2's margin overstates its own weight.} \textsc{shuffle\_trained}
scores $0.0167$, near the floor, so $+0.4893$ is a large number about a weak
control in absolute terms. The load-bearing evidence is the crossover in
Table~\ref{tab:l5b}, where the same policy $S$ \emph{beats} $R$ in the shuffled
world, showing $S$ is competent for the world it trained on rather than broken.

\paragraph{Other limits.} The comparator is the best fixed rule found under a
matched budget, not the best that exists. The policy was selected on development
data over three seeds (spread $0.133$); the matched comparator search is what
makes that fair. Local stability of the optimal mapping was inherited from
frozen calibration and not recomputed on confirmatory seeds, though the
bijection itself was recomputed by exhaustive search and recovered.

\section{Reproducibility}

Three protocol locks are preserved (v1.0, v1.1, v1.2), each pinning the
admissible commit, source-tree hash, config hash, dependency set and all three
seed lists, and each asserting field-by-field equivalence to its predecessor on
every scientific field. One aborted execution attempt is retained rather than
deleted: it stopped at the manifest-validation gate on a lock-resolution defect,
two gates before any comparative metric is computed. Every run carries a
manifest recording resources, diagnostics and the leakage verdict; 131 tests
gate the repository.

\section{Conclusion}

On a synthetic benchmark where different observations genuinely deserve
different storage depths, a policy reading only decision-time-legal features
learns a write-depth allocation that beats the best fixed rule found under an
equal search budget, and the advantage is attributable to having learned from a
genuine prefix-to-future-utility relationship rather than to capacity, compute,
privileged context, or budget allocation. This is one controlled result on one
toy with five seeds. Whether write-depth routing survives contact with a
language model is open, and the design constraints that a sibling track's
engineering pilot has already imposed on that question are recorded in
\texttt{experiments/EXP-002-DESIGN.md}.
